# ToB Sales and Career Capital

## Start from the customer's buying logic

For each industry or company, build a Sales Knowledge Card:

- offering and business outcome;
- customer segments and use scenarios;
- pains, trigger events, and cost of inaction;
- user, technical evaluator, procurement, finance, economic buyer, and veto holders;
- budget source and buying process;
- typical deal size and sales cycle, only when sourced or clearly labeled as an estimate;
- decision criteria, competitors, objections, proof required, delivery risks, and renewal/expansion motion.

Translate industry knowledge into a complete ToB motion:

`discover need → qualify → design solution → prove value → negotiate/procure → manage delivery → collect payment → retain/expand`

## Diagnose the real role

Judge responsibilities, accounts, territory, quota ownership, and support—not the title.

| Role pattern | Strong version | Weak version |
|---|---|---|
| Key account | owns strategy and senior stakeholders for valuable accounts | reactive order maintenance |
| Solution sales | discovers needs, shapes solution/ROI, orchestrates technical teams | presales coordination without commercial ownership |
| Regional sales | owns a defined market, revenue, accounts, and route-to-market | broad territory with no base or resources |
| Channel sales | recruits, enables, governs, and grows productive partners | price/quotation administration |
| Project sales | owns pursuit through delivery and collection | lead handoff only |
| BD | creates a new market, partnership, or repeatable motion | list building and cold outreach only |
| Overseas sales/trade | enterprise or channel ownership with market depth | inquiry response, quotation, follow-up, and low-price trading |

Good JD signals include defined territory/customer, revenue responsibility, discovery/solution work, commercial negotiation, partner/account ownership, cross-functional delivery, and local support.

Risk signals include vague `develop overseas markets`, unlimited geographies, high commission as the main attraction, no product/customer definition, pure lead targets, quotation-heavy duties, no presales/service, or a target disconnected from an existing base.

## Score full-cycle ownership

Evaluate whether the role participates in:

1. account/market planning;
2. prospecting and qualification;
3. discovery and stakeholder mapping;
4. solution/ROI design;
5. proposal, tender, and negotiation;
6. contracting, delivery, and issue management;
7. payment collection;
8. retention, upsell, reference creation, or partner growth.

More stages are not automatically better if the company provides no support. Distinguish ownership from being left alone.

## Evaluate three-year career capital

Ask what durable assets remain:

- **Customer capital:** accepted-market enterprise accounts and senior relationships.
- **Industry capital:** domain, use-case, competitor, and buying-process knowledge.
- **Deal capital:** large/complex deal, tender, negotiation, and collection experience.
- **Technical capital:** discovery, solution, ROI, and integration literacy.
- **Channel capital:** partner recruitment, enablement, forecasting, and governance.
- **International capital:** business English, cross-cultural work, local-market judgment.
- **Leadership capital:** territory planning, forecast, resource orchestration, mentoring.

State likely next roles and adjacent industries. Prefer paths toward Regional Sales Manager, APAC/Europe sales leadership, strategic KA, solution sales, or high-quality channel leadership.

## Adapt to the person

Use the user's actual education, languages, technical base, sales experience, location, mobility, graduation status, and risk tolerance. Separate:

- ideal long-term target;
- realistic immediate entry;
- bridge role needed to close the gap.

Never penalize or reward an unknown background variable. Mark it unknown and show how the conclusion changes under plausible conditions.

## Interview reality check

Generate questions that resolve hidden economics:

- exact territory and account ownership;
- last year's territory revenue and this year's target;
- active customers/partners and new-versus-existing revenue mix;
- average deal size, cycle, win rate, and collection responsibility;
- local presales, service, marketing, and delivery support;
- predecessor turnover, ramp period, commission rules, and promotion path.

Do not present unknown answers as facts.
