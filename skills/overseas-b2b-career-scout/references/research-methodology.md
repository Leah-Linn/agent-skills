# Research Methodology

## Use current research for unstable facts

Browse whenever evaluating current companies, markets, hiring, financials, offices, leaders, policies, or product portfolios. State the research cutoff date. Prefer primary evidence and cite claims near the text they support.

## Evidence hierarchy

For industry/company claims, prefer:

1. audited annual/interim reports, exchange filings, regulator records;
2. official regional/company/product/career pages;
3. government, standards bodies, trade associations, tenders, and certification databases;
4. customer/partner case studies and official event/exhibitor records;
5. reputable specialist research and business media;
6. major job platforms and professional profiles as corroborating signals.

Use the separate recruiting hierarchy in `job-market-intelligence.md` for openings.

Triangulate consequential claims such as market leadership, overseas presence, customers, and expansion. Treat a company's own marketing claim as attributed unless independently verified.

## Maintain an evidence ledger

For each important decision claim, record internally:

- claim;
- source and publication date;
- evidence date/period;
- direct fact, attributed claim, or inference;
- confidence and unresolved contradiction.

Resolve name collisions, parent/subsidiary relationships, brand versus legal entity, fiscal periods, currencies, and organic versus acquisition-driven growth.

## Separate fact, inference, and unknown

Use clear language:

- `Confirmed:` directly supported by current evidence.
- `Inference:` reasoned from cited signals; explain the bridge.
- `Unknown:` material data not found or not public.

Never fabricate revenue, offices, customers, quotas, hiring plans, salaries, deal sizes, or links. Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence.

## Research breadth and stopping rule

For a full industry scan:

1. establish chain structure from at least two credible perspectives;
2. search each high-value node for leaders, challengers, and hidden champions;
3. verify accepted-market presence for shortlisted companies;
4. research recruiting across official sources before platforms;
5. stop when additional searches produce no material new company category, market signal, or viable opening, and state important gaps.

Avoid false completeness. Describe the company/job pool as a researched shortlist, not the entire market, unless the dataset truly supports completeness.

## Compare on aligned periods

When comparing companies, align reporting periods and metric definitions. If not possible, disclose the mismatch. Do not rank companies from incomparable figures without caveat.

## Cite direct links

Use direct pages rather than search-result links. For recruiting, link the job detail or application page whenever available; otherwise mark it as a general official portal and do not call the role verified.
