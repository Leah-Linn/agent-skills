# Industry and Company Research

## Convert an industry map into a sales map

Start with a flexible chain:

`resources/materials → core components → equipment/products → systems/solutions → channels/integrators → end customers`

For each meaningful node, capture:

| Question | Career meaning |
|---|---|
| What is actually sold? | Product, service, platform, project, or outcome |
| Who buys, uses, influences, and approves? | Decision chain and stakeholder complexity |
| Why does the customer buy now? | Revenue, cost, risk, compliance, capacity, reliability, or strategic value |
| How is it sold? | Direct, channel, tender, solution, project, land-and-expand, or transactional |
| What makes a vendor hard to replace? | Technology, certification, integration, service, switching cost, ecosystem, or trust |
| What does a salesperson learn? | Technical discovery, ROI, negotiation, channel, delivery, and account planning |

Express technical features as customer value. For example, do not stop at higher cycle life; connect it to lifetime economics, reliability, or reduced downtime.

## Judge the cycle

| Stage | Signals | Sales implication |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | pilots, education, uncertain budgets | high BD risk; good only with support and patient capital |
| Growth | orders, capex, geographic expansion, hiring | preferred entry stage |
| Mature | stable leaders, standardized products | KA/channel value; watch commoditization |
| Decline/price war | falling margins, consolidation, weak cash collection | downgrade unless a premium niche is defensible |

Do not equate stock momentum with hiring quality. Use capital-market attention only as a discovery clue.

## Discover companies by layer

For each high-value node, find:

- **Leaders:** scale, established brand, mature ecosystem.
- **Second-tier leaders/challengers:** credible product, faster expansion, more ownership potential.
- **Hidden champions:** narrow category strength, defensible technology/certification, recognized B2B customers, low consumer awareness.

Do not restrict discovery to A-shares. Consider listed and unlisted Chinese firms, specialist private companies, global niche vendors, and relevant local competitors. Company status must be current and sourced.

## Analyze overseas expansion maturity

Classify each company:

1. **Export only:** domestic team ships through traders or opportunistic agents.
2. **Channel expansion:** repeatable distributors/dealers/partners, but limited local operation.
3. **Localized operation:** entity, local sales/service, inventory or delivery capability, customer success.
4. **Mature regional business:** established revenue, accounts, teams, ecosystem, and leadership path.

Best career risk/reward often lies in a credible stage 2 moving to 3, or stage 3 scaling to 4. Verify rather than infer from one office address.

## Inspect business quality for sales work

Use multi-year evidence where available:

- revenue and overseas revenue direction;
- gross margin and price pressure;
- operating cash flow and receivables;
- contract liabilities/orders/backlog when meaningful;
- customer concentration and customer quality;
- sales expense/headcount and geographic expansion;
- product competitiveness, certifications, and local service.

Growth is not sufficient. A company with fast reported sales but worsening collections, extreme concentration, weak support, or commodity pricing may be a poor platform for a salesperson.

## Run the anti-popularity check

After identifying obvious names, deliberately search adjacent components, control systems, industrial software, testing, connectors, cooling, power, integration, and other enabling layers. Ask whether a less-famous node offers higher accepted-market demand, stronger product moat, better customers, and more complete sales work.
